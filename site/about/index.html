antifeed

one good read a day. no scroll, no bait.

Feeds are machines for spending your attention. They're very good at it — infinite, algorithmic, engineered so there is always one more thing. The reading they produce is wide, shallow, and gone by Tuesday.

antifeed is the opposite machine. Once a day it surfaces one piece worth your full attention — picked by a curator that knows exactly who it's picking for, and pitched with a hook that tells you honestly why this one, today. When you're done, you're done. There is nothing to pull down, nothing to refresh. The absence of more is the feature.

A few convictions baked in:

Depth beats volume. One great essay actually read beats forty tabs sort-of skimmed. A great piece from 2019 you never read outranks a mediocre one from this morning — publication dates are feed-brain.

The discussion is part of the text. When a pick comes through Hacker News, the thread rides along. Comments by practitioners are a gold mine; pretending otherwise wastes half the value.

Memory, not metrics. Star what mattered, tick what you read, skip what isn't for you. Those marks exist so the reading compounds — not to feed a streak counter. There are no streaks. Missing a day costs nothing but the read.

Read, don't tweak. The machinery stays deliberately boring — a static page, one JSON file, a curation script — so the habit, not the system, is where the effort goes.
